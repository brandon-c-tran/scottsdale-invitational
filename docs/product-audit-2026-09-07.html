Field Day · Product directionSeptember 7, 2026

Let the app carry more of the weekend

The biggest opportunity is to remove coordination work from Brandon and his best men while making each player’s next move obvious. These are proposed product changes, grounded in the audit; they have not been built.

Product directionAudit · 22 findingsDetailed proposal

Mobile players and commissioners, plus the shared TV. Touch targets, outdoor legibility and interruption recovery stay in scope.

Build first

Build the shared game flow, preparation handoff and delegated scoring together. Use each result for the room reveal and personal receipt. Treat the poker finale as the next complete workflow.

Proposed experiences

5 proposals
S1

Give the three commissioners a shared run sheet

Keep one live contest and a separate preparing lane. The next event has a named person handling setup, confirmed participants and crew, and a ready handoff. Promoting it makes it public across phones and TV together.

In practice

While Brandon runs 8-Ball, one best man prepares volleyball’s draft and another sets up the station. Preparation stays backstage. When the current event ends, the prepared event is ready to announce.

Impact, scope and validation+

Impact

Less hunting through Events, duplicated setup and verbal checking. Hosts can work ahead without hijacking what the room sees.

What changes

Preparation ownership, readiness and handoff are new. The existing next-action button stays useful inside the live event; another button would not deliver this change.

Guardrails

Keep one official betting market initially. Handoffs are visible, not hard locks that stop another commissioner helping.

How to test it

Measure time from an event result to the next game being ready, repeated setup actions and commissioner navigation outside the live event.

S2

Make event crew able to run their assigned game

Give one named scorekeeper or event official permission to record results for that event. Their phone opens directly to its actual bracket, heat or standings entry. An accepted result settles chips and advances the next contest.

In practice

Evan is the 8-Ball scorekeeper. He taps the winning team at the table; the next matchup opens on everyone’s phones. Brandon can keep playing instead of receiving and entering the result.

Impact, scope and validation+

Impact

Removes the repeated finish-game → find-a-commissioner → explain-result → wait-for-entry handoff. A crew role becomes real participation.

What changes

Scoped event authority is new. Crew currently have a label; commissioner-only entry still requires the hosts to operate every result.

Guardrails

Delegate once to a trusted person, with a visible recorder and correction trail. Commissioners retain takeover and correction. Use a safe correction path when later bets exist; do not introduce mandatory opponent approvals or a general guest inbox.

How to test it

Measure the share of contests completed without commissioner input and the number of result corrections. Pilot with 8-Ball and one multi-qualifier heat event.

S3

Make Home my place in the current game

Use one live event destination reached from Home, Events and Bets. It presents my playing, waiting, crew, captain or spectator state, with the relevant action on the actual game board. Keep the full leaderboard visible below.

In practice

During someone else’s play-in, Brandon sees the current matchup plus “Chinh is your partner; semifinal 2.” When that matchup becomes current, his own-team chip target and play instructions become primary. A spectator sees both betting sides for the same contest.

Impact, scope and validation+

Impact

A player can answer what is happening, what they need to do and what comes next in one glance. The app carries context between betting, playing and results.

What changes

A complete personal participation lifecycle is the change: queued, up now, qualified, eliminated, crew and finished. Patching one missing partner label would cover only one state.

Guardrails

Keep the familiar navigation and direct chip interaction. Make Bets a useful view of the same contest and personal positions, rather than a second event controller. Readiness prompts should not hold up a game for thirteen taps.

How to test it

Observe whether a waiting player, current competitor and crew member can identify their next job within ten seconds without leaving Home.

S4

Make every result a shared moment and a personal receipt

One official result drives a short room reveal and a personalized phone receipt: event award, won/lost bets, new chip total and rank movement. Save that same result as an expandable event recap and build the weekend recap from those records.

In practice

A match ends: TV shows the winners and the board moving. Evan’s phone shows his +100 winning bet and new balance. After the volleyball final, teammates see their team award; someone who missed the reveal can reopen it from Events.

Impact, scope and validation+

Impact

Makes the competition legible and memorable. Players see why their chips moved and the group gets the fun of a shared outcome.

What changes

Animations already exist. Connecting awards, wagers, personal change and a durable recap is the added experience. It also provides the basis for factual weekend highlights and rivalry records later.

Guardrails

Use the group’s actual results and Brandon’s voice. Keep receipts brief and nonblocking, TV optional, and the current game visible between moments. Add photos only if there is an easy real capture workflow.

How to test it

Ask players to explain their latest chip change and find a result they missed. Check that the room can follow the outcome without a commissioner narrating the bookkeeping.

S5

Give the finale its own complete table workflow

Poker moves through handing out earned stacks, live play, collecting final counts, reconciliation and the champion. Players get their own table action; commissioners divide count review; TV shows the matching room phase.

In practice

At handout, each phone shows the exact chips to take. During play it shows blinds and bust status. At final counting, three commissioners can split the remaining players and see what is missing before posting together.

Impact, scope and validation+

Impact

The physical-table handoff becomes a coordinated conclusion to the weekend, with fewer verbal checks and accidental phase changes.

What changes

An explicit shared table workflow replaces the current inference that any personal count means everyone is finishing. Existing earned stacks, counter and poker rules are retained.

Guardrails

Personal counting must not end play for everyone. Keep cards physical; do not expand into an online poker engine. Large count discrepancies need deliberate review.

How to test it

Rehearse the full handout-to-champion path with the three hosts and players, checking that every role knows whether play or final collection is underway.

22 findings across the product

Keep the direct chip board, visible leaderboard, player cards and draw/draft motion. Repair blocked journeys and conflicting role state first; consolidate the live event and result models; give poker a deliberate playing-to-counting handoff.

P1Quick Draw presents rejected attempts as finishedFD-02Player+

Problem

The game enters ‘Your draw’ before the server accepts the run. Errors appear behind the full-screen game, leaving participants waiting for a run that was never saved.

Reproduce

Both browser participants waited over 38 seconds after Draw, then tapped. The server rejected ‘Bad time’; both screens showed a time and ‘Waiting on’ the other player. A too-fast automated 69 ms run showed the same false completion. Reopening the game was necessary.

Recommended change

Model captured, saving, accepted and rejected attempts explicitly. Preserve a valid captured run on transport retry; an invalid time needs an honest retry state. Put recovery inside the game.

Acceptance

Rejected or unacknowledged runs never look saved. Retrying a valid run does not grant a better reaction attempt; accepted retries are harmless. A player can always exit.
